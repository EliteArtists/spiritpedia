Editorial flag — BOOKS, misattribution risk. Four titles commonly sold as "Napoleon Hill" books were excluded because he did not write them. Do not let them into the catalogue:
* How to Attract Men and Money — written by Rosa Lee Beeland (Hill's fourth wife).
* Believe and Achieve — written by Samuel A. Cypert; published by the Foundation, marketed under W. Clement Stone's name.
* Wisdom for Winners — written by Jim Stovall; an official Foundation publication, not Hill's writing.
* PMA Science of Success — a Hill/Stone home-study course, not a trade book.

Overall editorial read. Napoleon Hill's estate is unusually well-organised on the publishing side and unusually thin on the teaching side. The book catalogue is deep and every retail link resolves. But there is no video archive, no podcast, no free digitised primary archive, no dated events since 2015, and the education pages haven't been touched in over a year. The Napoleon Hill Institute supplies most of the living, paid programming — and its licence, while very likely real, is nowhere publicly documented. That is the one decision I'd put in front of you before publishing sections 3 and 4.
One further editorial note: Hill sits at the edge of the Spiritpedia frame. He is a New Thought–adjacent success philosopher rather than a healer, and his explicitly metaphysical work is confined to a handful of titles — Outwitting the Devil, You Can Work Your Own Miracles, Napoleon Hill's Golden Rules and the Master Key material. The subject slugs above lean on manifestation, law-of-attraction, consciousness and soul-purpose accordingly, and use mysticism, hermeticism and shadow-work only where the text genuinely earns them.
