* Name: George Vithoulkas (Prof. Georgios Vithoulkas)
* Slug: george-vithoulkas
* Bio:
George Vithoulkas was born in Athens in July 1932 and came to homeopathy by an unlikely route — trained as a civil engineer, he encountered the subject while working in South Africa and went on to study it formally in India, qualifying in 1966. He returned to Greece and spent the following decades doing something almost no one else was doing at the time: teaching classical homeopathy as a rigorous, systematic discipline, with live case-taking in front of students, in the manner of a teaching hospital.
He is the founder of the International Academy of Classical Homeopathy (IACH), established in 1995 on the small Sporadic island of Alonissos, where practitioners from around the world travel for on-site seminars. Since 2010 the Academy has also run a two-year e-learning programme now delivered in eleven languages. His writings — The Science of Homeopathy, A New Model for Health and Disease, the Essence of Materia Medica, and the ongoing multi-volume Materia Medica Viva — have been translated into 36 languages and remain standard reference works in homeopathy training worldwide.
His teaching centres on what he calls the "Levels of Health" model: a framework in which he proposes that a person's vitality can be graded, and that the practitioner's task is to read the direction a case is moving in rather than chase individual complaints. In 1996 he received the Right Livelihood Award (often called the "Alternative Nobel Prize") for, in the jury's words, his contribution to the revival of homeopathic knowledge and to training homeopaths to high standards. He holds honorary professorships at several universities in Eastern Europe and continues to direct the Academy and lecture at 94.

4. His One-Minute Healer and Steps to Healing titles are general wellbeing rather than homeopathy, and are the two books in this entire audit that sit most comfortably in Spiritpedia's wider subject space.
5. The Indiegogo campaign referenced in two videos is from 2015 and closed. The links in the titles are dead.

AUDIT COMPLETE — CROSS-COHORT NOTES
Three issues recurred across all five and are probably platform-level rather than healer-level:
The resource-type vocabulary doesn't fit this field. meditation / download / mini_course / workshop / practice has no slot for a browsable reference library, an article archive, or a podcast — which is what most of these healers' free output actually is. Same for course / retreat / download / membership, which has no slot for a one-to-one consultation. I forced the nearest fit in every case and flagged each one.
Condition-named content is unavoidable in homeopathy. Four of the five have their most popular material named after illnesses. Spiritpedia's framing rule can govern Spiritpedia's own words, but the brief also requires exact titles. Those two requirements collide in this vertical in a way they presumably don't in, say, meditation or sacred-geometry. Worth a standing policy rather than five separate judgement calls.
Marketplace coverage is genuinely poor for Indian-published and self-published titles. Sankaran and Farokh Master together account for over a hundred books, of which a small minority have Goodreads pages and almost none are on World of Books. The NULLs in this audit are real findings, not gaps in the research. A direct_purchase_url fallback field would recover most of them.
