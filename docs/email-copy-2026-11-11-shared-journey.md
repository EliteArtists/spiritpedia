# Sending and stopping rules

- From and replies: love@spiritpedia.co, the Spiritpedia team. Greeting:
  “Hello,”. Plain text with no images. Use the final practitioner
  sign-off exactly: “With love, The Spiritpedia team”.

- Keep the existing schedule: days 0, 21, 42, 63, 84, 105, 126 and 147
  after Start. Stop when the listing is claimed, the recipient asks to
  stop or asks for removal, or the email bounces. Do not require a
  further sign-in after claiming. End after C8.

- Use only published business or organisation inboxes as requested.
  Record the source and recipient type for the sending eligibility
  process. A public address alone must not be treated as proof of
  consent or permission to send.

- Every message identifies Spiritpedia, the free public listing and why
  the team is writing, supplies a claim route and correction route, and
  provides an opt-out. Preserve each corresponding practitioner opt-out
  sentence exactly. C1 and C8 additionally say “To stop these emails,
  simply reply.”

- Do not promise bookings, traffic, health outcomes or permanent free
  participation. The interview is optional and independent of claiming.
  Keep replies monitored and stop/removal requests effective.

- Before changing the implementation, investigate and report on
  docs/emails.md, template selection by listing type, the claim flow,
  the scheduler and suppression controls. Template installation must not
  start live journeys.

## Use only what is actually on the listing

For a channel, say “videos” when only videos are listed, “episodes” when
only episodes are listed, and “videos and episodes” when both are
listed. Do not promise a podcast episode collection when the listing has
only an external podcast link.

Mention linked teachers only when at least one teacher relationship is
present. Mention catalogue books only when books are actually associated
with the publisher. Generate these sentences from stored relationships,
not inferred affiliations. Do not imply a linked teacher is a member of
staff or that the institution endorses Spiritpedia.

If the content relationships are absent, use this complete fallback for
\[listing detail\]: “Your listing introduces your \[listing kind\] and
gives people a place to find out more about your work.” Apps always use
their own sentence unless further content relationships have been
verified.
